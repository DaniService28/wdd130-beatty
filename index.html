Why we write Poems

We write poems to make silence speak. To give shape to what the heart feels but cannot explain. Poetry emerges when words become too heavy for plain sentences — when emotions demand rhythm, pause, and metaphor to exist.
Poems are small revolutions: quiet, but powerful. They remind us that vulnerability is strength, that brevity can be thunderous, and that the intangible — love, grief, awe — deserves to be named with care. Writing a poem is not an act of showing off; it is an act of listening. To life. To pain. To beauty.

The Power of Poetic Language

Poetry is the language of emotion distilled into its purest form. Where prose may explain, poetry evokes. A poem doesn’t tell you how the author feels — it lets you feel it yourself. Through deliberate choice of rhythm, silence, and imagery, poets craft miniature worlds out of metaphor and meaning.
A single line can hold centuries of grief or the joy of a morning breeze. It may be simple on the surface, but layered beneath are questions, memories, and universal truths. It speaks across generations and languages, whispering into the quiet corners of the reader’s heart.

Words that Endure

Master of theatre and eternal metaphor.

William Shakespeare

- “To be, or not to be: that is the question.” - “All the world’s a stage, and all the men and women merely players.” - “Some are born great, some achieve greatness, and some have greatness thrust upon them.” - “Love looks not with the eyes, but with the mind; and therefore is winged Cupid painted blind.”

Chronicler of magical realism.

Gabriel García Márquez

- “What matters in life is not what happens to you but what you remember and how you remember it.” - “It is not true that people stop pursuing dreams because they grow old; they grow old because they stop pursuing dreams.” - “Nobody deserves your tears, but whoever deserves them will not make you cry.” - “There is always something left to love.”

Keen observer of society and the human heart.

Jane Austen

- “It is a truth universally acknowledged, that a single man in possession of a good fortune, must be in want of a wife.” - “There is no charm equal to tenderness of heart.” - “I declare after all there is no enjoyment like reading!” - “A lady’s imagination is very rapid; it jumps from admiration to love, from love to matrimony in a moment.”

Humorist and satirist with down-to-earth wisdom.

Mark Twain

- “The secret of getting ahead is getting started.” - “Kindness is the language which the deaf can hear and the blind can see.” - “The two most important days in your life are the day you are born and the day you find out why.” - “Never argue with stupid people, they will drag you down to their level and then beat you with experience.”

Innovator of inner monologue and feminine introspection.

Virginia Woolf

- “A self that goes on changing is a self that goes on living.” - “Books are the mirrors of the soul.” - “You cannot find peace by avoiding life.” - “No need to hurry. No need to sparkle. No need to be anybody but oneself.”

Poet of labyrinths, books, and time.

Jorge Luis Borges

- “I have always imagined that Paradise will be a kind of library.” - “Time is the substance I am made of.” - “Don’t talk unless you can improve the silence.” - “Being with you and not being with you is the only way I have to measure time.”
